Quitting

For the thing you're trying to stop

Alcohol, vaping, doomscrolling, whatever it is — Lotara counts the days since your last, and shows you when the pull is strongest: which hours, and what usually comes before it.

When it hits, one tap opens the moment before the slip. A breathing orb, the reason you wrote down when you were clear-headed, and the swap you rehearsed. Two honest ways out — you rode it out, or logged, not judged. Never a shaming wall.

It reads your day before it suggests anything

Apple Health brings in last night's sleep, your resting heart rate and your movement. Your mood check-in and what you wrote in your journal sit alongside them.

Lotara reads all four together — not one number in isolation — and says what it noticed and what fits today, in plain words. It never overclaims. When there isn't enough to go on, it says so.

Journal

Write it down, and it starts connecting

Type it or speak it — voice entries transcribe themselves. Lotara reads what you wrote and reflects the patterns back: which days come up heavy, what tends to precede a hard evening, what you keep returning to.

It never scores you and it never lectures. Entries stay yours, on your device and your private cloud, and nothing is sold or shared.

Mood

One word a day becomes a map

The check-in takes a second: pick the word that fits. Over a month it turns into something you can actually read — what lifts you, what costs you, and which days you tend to struggle.

Because it sits next to your sleep, your heart rate and your streaks, Lotara can line them up and tell you what it noticed rather than leaving you to guess.

Some days the honest answer is "low." That's a data point, not a failure.
