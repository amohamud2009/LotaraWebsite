Quitting

For the thing you're trying to stop

Alcohol, vaping, doomscrolling, whatever it is — Lotara counts the days since your last and watches for the shape of the pull: which hours it clusters in, and what tends to come right before it.

That's the part most apps skip. A streak counter tells you how you've done. Lotara tells you when you're about to be tested — your peak window, learned from your own weeks — early enough to do something about it.

When it hits, one tap opens the moment before the slip: a breathing orb, the reason you wrote down when you were clear-headed, and the swap you rehearsed.

Two honest ways out — you rode it out, or logged, not judged. Never a shaming wall. At Serious support it can lock the tempting apps for the window too.

"Times you gave in, per week — down 73% since you began."

✦ Health & Lotara Intelligence

It reads your day before it suggests anything

Apple Health brings in last night's sleep, your resting heart rate and your movement. Your mood check-in and what you wrote in your journal sit alongside them.

The score only counts what actually reported in — four pillars today, six on a full day. Missing data is set aside, never scored against you.

Then Lotara reads all of it together, not one number in isolation, and says what it noticed in plain words.

It never overclaims. When there isn't enough to go on, it says so — which is rarer and more useful than a confident guess.

"Your resting rate was 66 bpm overnight, five above your average — a longer story gives you time to come down."

Sleep

Sleep is its own place at night

After dark the app changes. Sleep stops being a category in a library and becomes its own room — a night sky, a bedtime that knows your actual pattern, and a story chosen for the state you're in.

The stories are long-form and read by British narrators at a deliberately slow pace: Rain on a Tin Roof, The Night Train, The Cabin in the Snow. Rain, ocean or wind sits underneath at whatever volume you like, or none at all.

Which one you get isn't random. If your resting rate ran high or you're up past your bedtime, Lotara says so and picks accordingly — the longest one when you need time to come down, the shortest when it's already late.

It keeps playing when the screen goes dark, so nothing pulls you back out of it.

"You're up past your bedtime — this is the shortest one we have, at 13 min."

Journal

Write it down, and it starts connecting

Type it, or tap once and just talk — voice entries transcribe themselves, which matters on the nights when writing is the last thing you want to do.

Lotara reads what you wrote and reflects the patterns back: which days come up heavy, what tends to precede a hard evening, what you keep returning to.

It never scores you and it never lectures. There's no streak on your journal and no word count to hit.

Entries stay yours, on your device and your private cloud. Nothing is sold, and nothing is shared.

Mood

No words to pick. Just the orb.

The check-in takes a second and never asks you to name it first. Drag the orb and it takes the colour of how you feel — gold when you're joyful, calm blue when you're low — and the word arrives after, not before.

Because it sits next to your sleep, your heart rate and your streaks, Lotara can line them up and tell you what it noticed rather than leaving you to guess.

Some days the honest answer is "low." That's a data point, not a failure.
